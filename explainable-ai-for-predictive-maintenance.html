Explainable Artificial Intelligence for Predictive Maintenance Applications

Major Impacts

  • Practical XAI framing: Demonstrates how explanation quality matters in high-stakes maintenance decisions, not just raw classifier metrics.
  • Reusable dataset: Provides a realistic synthetic predictive maintenance dataset (10,000 rows) for benchmarking and education.
  • Method comparison: Compares explainable models (small decision trees) with model-agnostic explanatory interfaces (normalized feature deviation).
  • Actionable trade-off: Shows high-quality explanations can be sparse, while lower-fidelity explanations can be consistently available.

ELI5

Think of a machine doctor. It's not enough for the doctor to just say, "The machine will break." You want to know why. This paper tests two ways of giving that "why": one gives clearer reasons but sometimes says nothing; the other always gives a reason, but it can be less precise.

Explained to New Devs

The author builds a synthetic maintenance dataset with realistic signals (temperature, speed, torque, tool wear, product type) and multiple failure modes. A strong ensemble classifier predicts failure, then two explanation strategies are evaluated on selected failure cases:

  • Small decision trees (easy to read, often higher-quality explanations)
  • Normalized feature deviations (model-agnostic, always available)

Result: decision trees produce better explanations when they fire, but can fail to explain some cases. Feature-deviation explanations are usually "partially useful" but reliably present.

Explained to Senior Devs

This work separates predictive performance from local interpretability. The base model (bagged trees) handles an imbalanced setting (~3.39% failures) with weighted misclassification cost. Explanations are then generated either by constrained surrogate-style trees (max 4 nodes across feature subsets) or by normalized per-feature deviations. Quantitative scoring reveals a precision-coverage trade-off: tree explanations can be semantically richer but have non-trivial abstention; deviation-based interfaces offer stable coverage with lower explanation fidelity. The paper recommends selecting one explanation mode at a time to avoid cognitive conflict and suggests future augmentation with LIME-like methods.

Why This Matters for Real Systems

  • Trust calibration: Operators need reasons for single predictions, not only aggregate validation scores.
  • UX over algorithm worship: Explanation availability and clarity are product concerns, not only model concerns.
  • Hybrid strategy potential: Use high-fidelity explanations first, then fall back to robust lower-fidelity explanations.

Citation

Matzka, S. (2020). Explainable Artificial Intelligence for Predictive Maintenance Applications. 2020 Third International Conference on Artificial Intelligence for Industries (AI4I), 69-74. DOI: 10.1109/AI4I49448.2020.00023.
