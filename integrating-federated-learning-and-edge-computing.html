Integrating Federated Learning and Edge Computing for Privacy-Preserving and Real-Time Predictive Maintenance in Industrial IoT Systems

Core Problem and Proposed Solution

Traditional centralized machine learning for industrial monitoring requires raw sensor data to be sent to a central server, creating privacy risk, communication bottlenecks, and latency that weakens real-time fault detection. This dissertation proposes a decentralized framework that combines Federated Learning (FL) and Edge Computing (EC):

  • Federated Learning (FL): Trains models locally on edge nodes and shares encrypted parameter updates instead of raw sensitive data.
  • Edge Computing (EC): Executes data processing and inference near the machine source to reduce latency and improve responsiveness.

Methodology

  • Research design: Mixed-methods exploratory sequential approach.
  • Dataset: AI4I 2020 Predictive Maintenance dataset.
  • Models evaluated: CNN, LSTM, and hybrid CNN-LSTM architectures.
  • FL algorithms compared: FedAvg, FedProx, FedOpt, FedNova, and FedDyn.
  • Environment: Python ecosystem with TensorFlow, PyTorch, and PySyft, plus Docker-based edge emulation for heterogeneous industrial nodes.

Learning Framework Breakdown

To help master the core ideas, this section explains the dissertation using multiple learning frameworks, moving from definitions to intuition, root-cause reasoning, and practical examples.

1) Glossary of Key Terms

  • Predictive Maintenance (PdM): Using data and AI to predict when a machine is likely to fail so teams can fix it before breakdown.
  • Industrial IoT (IIoT): A connected network of sensors, instruments, and machines in industrial environments that collect and share data.
  • Edge Computing (EC): Processing data near the machine or at a local server, rather than sending everything to a distant cloud center.
  • Federated Learning (FL): A decentralized training method where machines train models locally and only share model updates, not raw data.
  • Latency: The delay between detecting an event and making a decision. In industrial safety contexts, lower latency is critical.
  • Non-IID Data: Data that differs across machines and sites (for example, due to age, environment, or workload), which complicates global learning.
  • FedAvg (Federated Averaging): A federated algorithm that averages model updates from multiple edge nodes to produce a stronger global model.

2) ELI5 (Explain Like I am Five)

Imagine students in different countries trying to learn the best cookie recipe.

  • Old centralized way: Everyone mails cookies, recipes, and kitchen details to one far-away chef. It is slow, expensive, and exposes private information.
  • New FL + Edge way: Each student bakes locally, keeps secrets at home, and only shares lessons learned. The chef combines those lessons and returns a better shared guide for everyone.

This is the same idea in factories: each site keeps sensitive data local, learns quickly at the edge, and still benefits from global intelligence.

3) The 7 Whys (Root Cause Analysis)

Goal: Understand why this framework is important for the future of industry.

  1. Why do we need this framework? Current industrial AI systems are often too slow and risky for modern plants.
  2. Why are they risky? Centralized approaches collect sensitive data in one place, increasing breach and misuse risk.
  3. Why is slowness a problem? Cloud round trips introduce latency.
  4. Why does latency matter? In fast-moving machinery, even short delay can amplify safety and equipment damage risk.
  5. Why not keep everything on one local machine? A single machine only learns from local failures.
  6. Why is cross-site learning important? It helps detect rare failures that may not have happened locally yet.
  7. Why is this research a solution? It combines shared learning (FL) with low-latency local decision-making (EC) while preserving privacy.

4) Example Scenarios

Scenario A: Global car manufacturer

A company operates factories in Germany, the USA, and China and wants paint-defect detection models to improve jointly.

  • Privacy challenge: Sites cannot freely move proprietary production data across borders.
  • Framework benefit: Sites share learned updates, not raw images, so each factory gains from others without exposing protected data.

Scenario B: Off-shore oil rig

An oil rig has constrained satellite connectivity and requires immediate reaction to pressure spikes.

  • Latency challenge: Sending alerts to a remote cloud can be too slow for safe intervention.
  • Framework benefit: Edge inference can react around 0.002 ms per sample, enabling rapid shutdown actions before a cloud-only path would respond.

Key Findings and Insights

  • Best overall algorithm: FedAvg delivered the strongest balance with 94.7% test accuracy and 0.955 F1, within 1% of centralized learning.
  • Latency gains: FL + EC cut inference latency by up to 60%, averaging 0.002 ms per sample.
  • Privacy without major loss: Encrypted parameter exchange caused no statistically significant degradation (p > 0.05).
  • Model synergy: LSTM with FedAvg was most feasible for temporal dependency modeling and early failure forecasting.
  • Scalability: Stable convergence persisted across heterogeneous and non-IID edge nodes, supporting multi-plant deployment feasibility.

Practical Applications

  • Industry fit: High-value sectors such as automotive and oil and gas can reduce downtime and maintenance cost.
  • Regulatory alignment: Local data retention supports data protection regimes such as GDPR and CCPA.
  • Strategic advantage: Organizations can collaborate on model improvement while protecting proprietary operational data.
